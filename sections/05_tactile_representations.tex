\section{From Local Touch to Cross-Interface Representations}
\label{sec:representations}

\subsection{A hierarchy of representation scales}

Tactile models can be placed on a hierarchy from sensor-local appearance to
task-level physical relations (
\cref{fig:representation_hierarchy}). Progress at a lower level is enabling but
does not imply progress at every higher level. For example, a sensor-invariant
embedding may improve local contact classification while discarding absolute
orientation or force magnitude that a load-transfer model needs. Conversely, a
task-specific relational policy may model useful interactions while inheriting a
fragile local encoder.

\subsection{Local self-supervised and transferable touch}

Self-supervised tactile learning replaces expensive per-task labels with
objectives such as reconstruction, masked prediction, temporal consistency, and
instance discrimination. T-Dex couples pretraining to robotic play, showing that
unlabeled interaction can benefit downstream dexterity~\cite{guzey2023tdex}.
Sparsh scales self-supervision over vision-based tactile data and evaluates a
family of downstream tasks~\cite{higuera2025sparsh}. These approaches establish
that generic visual backbones are not enough and that tactile-specific pretraining
can be reused.

Transfer across sensors is harder because pixels encode different elastomers,
illumination, marker patterns, optics, and spatial scales. UniTouch learns unified
multimodal tactile representations~\cite{yang2024unitouch}; AnyTouch explicitly
addresses static and dynamic information across multiple visuo-tactile sensors
~\cite{feng2025anytouch}; T3 studies transferable Transformers
~\cite{zhao2024t3}; and UniT uses a unified tactile modelling approach
~\cite{xu2024unit}. Sparsh-X broadens the multisensory alignment setting
~\cite{higuera2025sparshx}, while TactAlign maps human tactile-glove features into
a robot tactile space for cross-embodiment transfer~\cite{tactalign2026}. The
common scientific question is not simply whether
embeddings transfer, but which tactile factors remain identifiable after
alignment. A sensor-invariant latent that collapses shear direction or scale may
be detrimental to load-path reasoning.

Whole-hand and morphology-aware models extend the unit of representation. HTT
uses heterogeneous tactile tokenization and masked learning~\cite{bi2026htt};
FTP-1 incorporates sensor morphology in a large tactile policy
~\cite{yuan2026ftp1}; HT-Bench supplies a whole-hand benchmark with egocentric
vision~\cite{huang2026htbench}. These works motivate explicit metadata for sensor
location, normal direction, link identity, and quality. In bimanual systems,
morphology-awareness must also prevent a shortcut in which ``left'' and ``right''
sensor IDs stand in for task role.

\subsection{Temporal tactile representations}

Touch is an event stream. Static contact appearance can be ambiguous: the same
deformation may correspond to loading or unloading, stable stick or the moment
before slip, depending on recent history. TactileVAD highlights geometric
aliasing in learned tactile dynamics~\cite{oller2023tactilevad}. Reactive control
systems increasingly split slow semantic reasoning from fast tactile reaction;
T-Rex uses tactile-reactive control at different time scales~\cite{niu2026trex},
while recent slow--fast visuo-tactile diffusion policies pursue a related control
principle. ViTacFormer also includes future-touch prediction as part of
cross-modal representation learning~\cite{heng2025vitacformer}.

HiTac-WAM makes the prediction target more explicit: candidate actions are
evaluated through a directed contact $\rightarrow$ deformation $\rightarrow$ slip
hierarchy, and persistent forecast error can trigger replanning
~\cite{xue2026hitacwam}. VT-MUSE instead learns a frozen sequential
visuo-tactile representation using cross-modal temporal alignment, masked-view
consistency, and a training-only posterior that sees the full tactile history
~\cite{xu2026vtmuse}. These are stronger controls than a single future-frame
decoder. They also expose distinct hypotheses: whether physical variables should
be ordered explicitly, and whether a deployable prior can retain information
learned from richer training-time histories.

Temporal modelling choices include fixed windows, recurrent states, temporal
convolution, causal Transformers, and state-space models. Their comparison is
often confounded by receptive field, parameter count, and action history. For
contact-transition evaluation, causal alignment is essential: a future tactile
frame may be a valid training target, but it must not leak into the deployed
encoder input. Reporting should distinguish synchronous state estimation,
$100$--$300$ ms forecasting, and closed-loop reaction.

Vision/proprioception-only models also strengthen the temporal null hypothesis.
TemporalFlow-VLA supervises ordered execution-history queries with training-only
robot-surface flow~\cite{yang2026temporalflowvla}; PredVLA updates a compact
recurrent latent through prediction-error inference
~\cite{sawada2026predvla}. Neither supplies distributed touch, so neither closes
the tactile relation gap. They are nevertheless important matched baselines: a
new tactile structure should outperform comparable history grounding and
prediction-error correction, not merely a memoryless visual policy.

\subsection{World models: supervision, conditioning, and feedback}

The September frontier separates three often-conflated uses of touch. ME-Dex 1.0
jointly predicts canonical tactile states, video, and actions with engineered
hand-region mappings~\cite{zhang2026medex}. Yet its observed-versus-zero-current-
touch intervention reports nearly identical Random scores (91.92\% and 91.74\%);
its Clean-to-Random comparison also uses zero current touch. With one training
seed and qualitative physical deployment, this supports predictive supervision
more strongly than a claim that the reported controller needs online tactile
feedback. Cross-source reconstruction after pretraining on those sources is also
different from held-out-sensor transfer.

DexTouch-WM conditions future tactile prediction on initial sensing and a future
pose sequence~\cite{qin2026dextouchwm}. Human data improve contact prediction
with robot data held fixed, but shared taxel layouts and anatomical pad embeddings
supply correspondence. This is an action-conditioned model rather than a
standalone observation-only policy; evaluator agreement and synthetic-training
utility must be separated because synthetic substitution sometimes worsens real
performance. UVTA similarly supplies a useful predictive-supervision comparator
~\cite{li2026uvta}: aligned human/robot touch improves real right-hand task
scores, but the tactile prediction head is not used for control. Its stage-wise
partial-credit scores must not be rewritten as binary success rates, and
bilateral hardware must not be mistaken for bimanual experimental evidence.

ContactWorld adds unusually relevant representation controls, including
latent-dimension/planning-cost matching, multiple seeds, and same-GelSight
hardware comparisons~\cite{zhang2026contactworld}. It supports a conditional
rather than universal benefit from touch. Goal observations are drawn from future
held-out demonstrations, and real ``tactile force fields'' are depth/marker-motion
proxies rather than calibrated three-dimensional force. Separate hardware models
and sensor changes in some comparisons preclude a zero-shot cross-sensor claim.
For a contact-flow model, the lesson is to distinguish preserved physical
information, predictive objective, goal information, and online feedback
necessity before attributing gains to explicit relations.

\subsection{Multimodal fusion and spatial anchoring}

Tactile measurements acquire meaning from proprioception and vision. Early or
late concatenation is a strong baseline because a sufficiently expressive
temporal model can learn cross-modal interactions. Cross-attention gives tactile
tokens selective access to image, state, or action tokens; ViTacFormer is a
prominent recent example~\cite{heng2025vitacformer}. The fair question is whether
a proposed structure improves over this baseline at equal observation, compute,
and temporal context.

Physical-property fusion adds another alternative. ViTacPhys predicts mass,
friction class, and stiffness from human visual--tactile demonstrations and uses
the estimates to condition grasping~\cite{liu2026vitacphys}. Such property tokens
can explain adaptation across objects without representing a cross-hand load
path. A fair system-level comparison should therefore include both property-
conditioned fusion and relation-conditioned fusion, and ask which failure modes
each resolves.

Spatial anchoring transforms local touch into a shared frame. SaTA anchors tactile
awareness using forward kinematics, enabling spatial reasoning beyond sensor
index~\cite{huang2025sata}. Object-centric approaches similarly normalize motion
with respect to the manipulated entity~\cite{chen2024objectcentric}. Anchoring is
particularly valuable for two hands because corresponding tactile features lie
in separate kinematic trees. Yet anchoring assumes that the relevant transform is
known or perceived; errors in link calibration and object pose should be tested,
not hidden.

The early-October evidence sharpens spatial and predictive controls further.
SimpleTouch combines a frozen pretrained tactile encoder with task-specific
VLA/expert learning and removes the future-touch branch at deployment
~\cite{yang2026simpletouch}. ``Without tactile-policy pretraining'' still uses
upstream encoder/VLA pretraining. Its main insertion comparison also differs in
observation refresh (25 versus 50 executed actions); matched-horizon ablations
are the cleaner representation comparison. VISTA supplies a pose-conditioned
spherical-fusion alternative~\cite{wong2026vista}. Its equivariance is defined for
selected finite groups and joint workspace rotations, with current end-effector
orientation observed. Single-seed, single-gripper evidence and differing sensor/
group settings across broad baselines limit stronger transfer claims. Both works
make execution horizon and observation refresh part of the matching budget,
alongside local encoder, data, history, and parameter count.

\subsection{Direct cross-hand completion evidence}

BiView-Touch changes the boundary of the representation gap: it learns bilateral
human tactile representations by geometry-conditioned cross-hand completion,
with static anatomical-region centroids and temporal/layout counterfactuals
~\cite{liang2026biviewtouch}. Its matched temporal, within-hand, and dense-fusion
comparisons, together with donor-context substitutions and region permutations,
provide direct evidence that contralateral context can be useful. Dense fusion
and cross-attention perform similarly in the principal readout comparison, so
this does not establish architectural superiority of geometry-conditioned
attention. Cross-hand
representation learning itself should therefore no longer be presented as absent.

The evidence is nevertheless narrower than robotic load-path identification.
The experiments are offline human sensing; several phase/contact labels derive
from the same tactile input, whereas wrist-motion labels come from independent
tracking. No closed-loop robot study is reported. The held-out buckle task tests
task transfer, but its handover/connection motifs occur elsewhere in training;
the split does not hold out a declared participant/edge topology. Static anatomy,
predictive dependence, object-mediated force transmission, and calibrated load
inference remain distinct claims. A robotic study should retain the cross-hand
completion and dense-fusion controls while adding independent interface loads,
participant/session holds-out, and repeated pretraining seeds.

Pretraining objectives privilege different information. Reconstruction preserves
pixel detail but may waste capacity on illumination or marker appearance. Masked
prediction encourages context use but can exploit spatial redundancy. Contrastive
alignment emphasizes shared content but can suppress modality-specific physical
signals. Future prediction encodes dynamics but may average multimodal outcomes.
Task reward discovers control-relevant features but can exploit spurious state or
demonstration regularities. No objective is universally correct; its sufficiency
must be judged against the intended quantities and topology.

For bimanual coordination, promising auxiliary targets include contact onset and
release, future interface occupancy, load-share ratio, future local wrench, slip
risk, and calibrated uncertainty. These are not merely extra labels. They test
whether the latent exposes a mechanism that a downstream policy could use. Where
labels are available only in simulation, privileged-to-observation distillation
can train an observable student, but evaluation must verify that the student does
not require privileged inputs at test time.

\subsection{Necessary baselines for representation claims}

A new structured encoder should be compared against more than raw concatenation.
At minimum, a matched study should include: proprioception-only; tactile-only;
concatenation with the same local encoder; cross-attention; a temporal Transformer
or recurrent model; a prediction-error recurrent model; a flat multi-head and a
directed tactile hierarchy; training-only temporal grounding; spatial anchoring
where poses are available; a static graph; the proposed inferred structure; and
an oracle structure used only as an upper bound. Parameter count, input history,
optimization budget, augmentations, and pretraining data must be controlled or
reported. Without these controls, an improvement may come from capacity,
temporal context, objective ordering, or privileged supervision rather than
representation structure.
